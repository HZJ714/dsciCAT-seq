**Figure 7.** The mature-oligodendrocyte-versus-precursor risk contrast replicates in independent human MS cohorts and in mouse EAE, and is specific to the oligodendrocyte-maturation axis. 

(A) Unit-level Δ1 = median scDRS score of mature oligodendrocytes − median score of OPC/COP precursors, computed within each donor (GSE277430, GSE118257, GSE231585), within each library (GSE301908) and within each mouse library (GSE250589, spinal cord, FACS GFP+ from Sox10-cre animals, lesion phase as labelled). Dots are units, bars are group medians, colours are MS (red), EAE (orange) and control (dark blue). The number above each column is the Mann–Whitney rank-sum P for that column (MS or EAE versus control); the “P =” prefix is omitted on the face to keep the 0.75 cm column pitch clear. Values: GSE277430 0.500, GSE118257 1.000, GSE301908 0.091, GSE231585 0.393; mouse Early 0.100, Peak 0.333, Late 0.857. 

(B) The same contrast ranked across 74 GWAS gene sets (MAGMA, 10 kb windows, 1,000 genes each) in the four human cohorts. Rows are the 74 gene sets ordered top-down by the mean of their four within-cohort ranks; columns are cohorts; colour is the rank of cohort-level Δ1 within that cohort (1 = strongest mature-oligodendrocyte contrast, 74 = weakest). Because Δ1 tracks the size of each gene set’s regulatory domain in every cohort (Spearman ρ of Δ1 with ln n_peak = −0.84 / −0.69 / −0.78 / −0.87; n_peak counted on public human snATAC GSE231584, GRCh38, 121,505 deduplicated peaks, TSS ± 100 kb), the ranks shown are those obtained after residualising Δ1 on ln n_peak, so that a gene set is not ranked ahead of another merely for covering more chromatin. On this basis MS ranks 1 / 1 / 3 / 1, Alzheimer’s disease 18 / 49 / 27 / 12 and schizophrenia 68 / 74 / 42 / 71 (GSE277430 / GSE118257 / GSE301908 / GSE231585). The unadjusted ranking (MS 2 / 4 / 7 / 2), the ±15% matched-window control (MS 2 / 3 / 5 / 2) and the ρ strip are reported in Fig S7. Ranks are always computed within a cohort and never from the transformed values in (C). 

(C) The same nuclei re-projected onto two further cell-identity axes: astrocyte−immune (median astrocyte − median immune) and neuron−glia (median neuron − median glia; glia = astrocyte + immune + oligodendrocyte + OPC + vascular), beside the oligodendrocyte-maturation axis of (A) and (B). Values are robust z of Δ, standardised within cohort × axis by median and MAD, so the four tiles share one y-axis; violins are kernel densities of the 74 gene-set values in that cohort × axis, evaluated only inside the visible window (truncated at −4.7 / +5.5), while ranks and interquartile boxes use all 74. Red dots are MS and dark-blue dots schizophrenia, each labelled #n with its rank on the axis it sits on. The single asterisk marks that axis, not that cohort: GSE231585 contains 62,911 nuclei of which 717 (1.1%) are annotated neurons, 4–133 per sample, so under the frozen rule that a group must contribute at least 10 nuclei to a per-unit median, two of its 15 samples drop out of the neuron−glia axis (13 units there, 14 on the other two axes). Nothing is subsampled; 717 is the annotated neuron pool of the cohort. The other cohorts’ neuron pools are 7–100-fold larger (GSE277430 76,436; GSE118257 5,031; GSE301908 ≤ 12,000 per lineage by the stratified draw), so no other tile carries a mark. 

(D) The 18 approved or in-trial MS drug targets shown out of 80 scored. Left block: within-cohort log2 odds ratio of detection in mature oligodendrocytes versus OPC; middle block: OL lineage versus all non-lineage cells; both over the three cohorts that carry the required annotations. Right block: the percentage of nuclei in which the gene is detected in GSE301908, shown separately for each cell population — a within-population detection rate, not a composition. The red box marks the single row that passes the pre-set rule (mature OL exceeds every non-lineage population by ≥1.0 percentage point and by ≥1.5-fold): S1PR5. Resolved per donor rather than pooled over nuclei, mature OL is still the population with the highest S1PR5 detection rate in 11 of 11 donors (one-sided sign test P = 4.9 × 10⁻⁴; median 8.3% in OL versus 3.7% in the strongest non-lineage population, 2.5-fold), while the full ≥1.0 pp / ≥1.5-fold margin is met in 6 of 11 donors — three of the five misses are driven by comparator pools of fewer than 35 nuclei and two (3.2% and 5.2% OL signal) are genuine low-margin donors. No other target passes the rule in any evaluable donor. The three drug-target columns (named in a product label / stated mechanism of action / used in MS through this target) describe the drug-target relation itself and are not localisation evidence. 

The statistical unit is the donor (library where a cohort is library-resolved), never the nucleus; n per unit, cohort sources, platforms and annotation provenance are given in Table S6 and Fig S7A. scDRS 1.0.2 (score_cell, n_ctrl = 200, fixed seeds per cohort).
